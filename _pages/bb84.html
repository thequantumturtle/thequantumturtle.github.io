Step 4 · Check for Eve

Reveal a sample of bits

To look for errors, Alice reveals selected bits from matching-basis rounds. These tested bits must be discarded.

An error may signal Eve or noise. No observed errors does not prove Eve was absent.

Finish · Candidate key

Keep the untested, matching-basis bits

This short simulation does not create a secure key. Real BB84 requires authenticated classical communication, error correction, privacy amplification, and physical quantum states.

State reference

Four states, two bases

|0⟩+ basis · bit 0
|1⟩+ basis · bit 1
|+⟩× basis · bit 0
|−⟩× basis · bit 1

Same basis: Bob gets Alice's encoded bit in the ideal case. Different basis: Bob's result is random.

What does Eve change?

When Eve measures in a random basis and resends a replacement, she can disturb the state. For an ideal intercept-resend attack, about 25% of matching-basis bits are wrong on average. Small samples vary.

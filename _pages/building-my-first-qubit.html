The Quantum Turtle · Build Journal

Building My First Qubit

What if you could explore a qubit with real buttons, knobs, and two small screens? We're turning our browser simulator into a physical learning tool.

V0.2 · Working breadboard prototype

Updated October 18, 2026 · Read the latest build update →

The controls now work

Two round screens, seven buttons, and two knobs now work together on an ESP32-S3. Turn one knob to change probability balance and the other to change relative phase. Press X, Y, Z, or H to apply a gate, Measure to get an outcome, or Reset to start again. A seventh button cycles through three color schemes without changing the state.

Each circle's filled area shows a measurement probability. Each hand shows its amplitude's phase. Together, the circles show one qubit. The board runs a classical simulation of a pure state, with gate animations that follow the browser version's math.

This is a working bench prototype with exposed wiring, not a finished children's toy.

A baseline we can build on

The V0.2 checkpoint records the working firmware, exact connections, tested behavior, and known limits. The knobs select discrete probability and phase settings. Brief button presses during an animation can still be missed.

Why make it physical?

Quantum computing can feel abstract. The goal is to give learners something they can touch while they explore what a quantum gate does and how measurement works.

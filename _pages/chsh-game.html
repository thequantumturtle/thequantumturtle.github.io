What makes the quantum game different?

The referee chooses independent, uniformly random bits x and y. Alice and Bob cannot communicate after receiving them. A classical strategy can win at most 75% of rounds on average. With shared entanglement and the right measurement settings, the maximum rises to cos²(π/8) ≈ 85.36% (Tsirelson's bound).

For the entangled |Φ⁺⟩ state and real measurement angles α and β, the correlation is E = cos(2(α−β)). The game samples individual outcomes from those quantum probabilities. It does not predetermine a win, and finite samples can be above or below the theoretical limits. Changing settings clears prior results so the observed rate always refers to one strategy.

This is a local browser simulation, not an experiment on quantum hardware. No account or physical separation is required: the game enforces the no-communication rule by generating each answer from only its local question and preselected strategy. Explore more Quantum Play Lab activities.

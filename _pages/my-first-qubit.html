Try this first

  1. Start at |0⟩. The blue circle is full.
  2. Press H. Both circles show a 50% chance.
  3. Press Z. The hands turn in opposite directions, but the chances stay the same.
  4. Press H again, then Measure. You will get 1.

What the board shows

Two circles show one qubit. Each circle's filled area shows the chance of measuring that result. Chance of 1 (θ) makes one circle grow as the other shrinks. Relative Phase (φ) sets the difference between the hands when you move the slider. Each hand shows the phase of its complex amplitude. Only the difference between the hands changes measurement behavior. When both hands rotate together, that is global phase, which does not affect measurement results. When a circle is empty, its clock hand has no direction. The phase setting stays saved until both circles have a size. Mathematically, relative phase is undefined when either amplitude is zero.

These sliders can show any pure state of one qubit. A mixed state needs a different model.

This browser toy is a first step toward a real wooden busy board. The real toy could use turning knobs and two small screens. It runs on your computer, not on quantum hardware.

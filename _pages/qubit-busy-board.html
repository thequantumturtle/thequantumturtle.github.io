The Qubit Busy Board

A little wooden toy for a very curious qubit. Press a gate, explore any pure qubit state with two controls, then measure what happens.

Try this first

  1. Choose |0⟩ and watch the pointer point up.
  2. Press H. The pointer moves to the equator and measurement becomes 50/50.
  3. Press Z. The probabilities stay the same, but the pointer changes direction.
  4. Press H again, then Measure. You should always get 1.

Little buttons, real quantum rules

The wooden frame, round viewing window, protected pointer and oversized buttons are a digital model for a future physical busy board. X flips a state, H changes its superposition, Z changes relative phase and Y rotates around a different axis. Each gate now shows a smooth rotation along its true Bloch-sphere path. Measurement animates a collapse to the observed pole. The displayed arrow is a 2D projection of the 3D Bloch vector, so distinct positions in depth can sometimes overlap. The balance and phase controls follow the standard Bloch angles: θ is measured down from the north pole and φ rotates around the equator. Phase has no observable effect at either pole.

Try it: press H, then Measure. Reset and try again. You may see 0 or 1, each with a 50% chance. Measurement updates the state to the outcome, so the result becomes certain until another gate changes it.

A simulation of ideal single-qubit gates and computational-basis measurement. No quantum hardware is used.
